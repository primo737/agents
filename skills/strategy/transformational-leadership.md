# Transformational leadership

# Instructional Prompt for Transformational Leadership

```sql
TRANSFORMATIONAL LEADERSHIP CONVERTER

I'll help you transform your task-based instructions into powerful outcome-focused directions using Dan Martell's transformational leadership framework from Chapter 11 of "Buy Back Your Time."

Simply share a current task instruction you give to your team, and I'll convert it into an empowering outcome that will give your team member the freedom to innovate while achieving better results. I'll also help you create a clear Definition of Done (DOD) so both you and your team member know exactly what success looks like.

Example:
YOUR TASK INSTRUCTION: "Check all emails for typos before sending them."
TRANSFORMED OUTCOME: "Ensure all customer communications maintain our professional standards."
DEFINITION OF DONE: "Communications are error-free, use appropriate tone, and represent our brand voice consistently."

Now, what task instruction would you like to transform?
```

When the user provides their task instruction, respond with:

1. A clear transformation of their instruction into an outcome-focused direction
2. A specific Definition of Done (DOD) that defines success criteria
3. The potential benefits of this new approach
4. A simple follow-up question about implementation

**Example Response Template:**

```diff
Here's your transformed leadership direction:

TASK INSTRUCTION: "Make 50 sales calls per day"
TRANSFORMED OUTCOME: "Generate 10 qualified leads each week"
DEFINITION OF DONE: "10 prospects who match our ideal customer profile, have budget authority, and have expressed interest in a demo or follow-up conversation."

BENEFITS:
- Empowers your team member to find the most effective approach
- Focuses on results rather than activity
- Encourages innovation and creative problem-solving
- Builds ownership and accountability
- Creates clarity around what success actually means

Would you like me to help you transform another instruction, or would you like suggestions on how to measure progress toward this outcome?
```

If they ask about Chapter 11 or want more background, offer this brief explanation:

```css
Chapter 11 of "Buy Back Your Time" introduces transformational leadership - a shift from telling people HOW to do things to telling them WHAT needs to be done. This approach gives your team ownership of solutions, freeing up your time while improving results.

As Dan Martell writes: "With transformational leadership, every time you give an instruction, you aren't telling others exactly how to do it, but what needs to get done. How? Well, that's up to them."
```

Otherwise, keep the process simple, practical, and focused on the immediate application of transformational leadership principles.

# Chapter 11 - Buy back your time

# Transformational Leadership

"Don’t tell people how to do things, tell them what to do and let them surprise you with their results." —General George Patton

In 2021, Adam, my Head of People, approached me with a challenge. "I need to hire eleven people in the next quarter. How on Earth am I going to do that?!" Adam was feeling the pressure. I checked to see if he just needed someone to listen, but he was looking for a solution. I directed him to his department's hiring manager—himself. "Adam, if you need help, I'll cancel meetings, brainstorm, and do whatever's necessary to support you. However, this is why I hired you," I told him. He smiled, stood up, and left the room. "I'll be back after I've thought more about it." But, like Miranda in Chapter 4, he never returned. Two months later, we had eleven outstanding new team members.

## Other People's Monkeys

What I did with Adam was straightforward: I transferred the ownership of the solution back to him. In Ken Blanchard's book, _The One Minute Manager Meets the Monkey_, Blanchard refers to these situations as "other people's monkeys."

With transformational leadership, you stop leading by instructing someone on how to do something and instead tell them what needs to be done. You set the outcomes and place the responsibility on them. In Chapters 7 and 9, we discussed assigning outcomes, whether broad, like email and calendar management, or specific, like running payroll for the week. Imagine not having to tell your salesperson how to sell the next client, your marketing leader how to create the next ad campaign, or your customer success manager how to handle a complaint. With transformational leadership, you provide the what, not the how. The method is up to them.

For example:
*   "Bethany, please spell-check blog posts before they go out" becomes "Bethany, the blog posts must always be error-free."
*   "Danielle, all employees need to take a class on the new GDPR updates" becomes "Danielle, we must be GDPR compliant."
*   "James, you need to make more sales calls" becomes "James, you need to hit one hundred sales."

James improves his sales skills, which he can use anywhere, even if he leaves your company. He exceeds his sales quota.

### Transactional vs. Transformational Management

*   **Transactional Management:** "Maloney, increase the menu prices." Result: Maloney raises prices by 10%.
*   **Transformational Leadership:** "Maloney, we need to make 10% more per square foot in every location." Result: Maloney creates seasonal offerings, attracting new customers and increasing revenue beyond 10%.

By setting outcomes instead of dictating methods, employees focus on results, not tasks. They contribute energy, not just skills, and start asking themselves if there's a better way instead of asking you how to do it. When you lead through desired outcomes, you're not in charge of the solution—they are. Ultimately, like Adam, they bring you possible solutions or solve problems independently.

## The Buyback Principle

"Don’t hire to grow your business. Hire to buy back your time."

Coaching your teammates is essential. Managers check off tasks and give orders, but leaders draw out the best in people. They see potential and make it a reality. Just as great athletes have coaches, you need to coach your team. You build the people, and they build the business.

### The CO-A-CH Framework

Coaching is often overused and misunderstood. With transformational leadership, you set goals and let individuals work towards them. When obstacles arise, you step in to help overcome them. Coaching involves small, pivotal conversations that adjust and guide progress.

Ensure each person is empowered to handle their responsibilities. Many leaders manage by directing, checking, and instructing, leading to a "tell-check-next" cycle. Instead, embrace transformational leadership by setting outcomes, measuring progress, and coaching to success. This approach allows employees to grow and make decisions with minimal involvement from you.

### Feedback and Productivity

Feedback is crucial for productivity. A lack of communication can become a cultural issue, spreading throughout the organization. Encouraging issues to surface saves time and energy. Matt Mochary, author of _The Great CEO Within_, provides a model for fostering open feedback. By creating an environment where feedback flows freely, you retain top talent and reduce turnover costs.

In summary, transformational leadership empowers employees to take ownership of their roles, fostering innovation and growth. By focusing on outcomes and providing guidance when necessary, you build a resilient and dynamic team.
