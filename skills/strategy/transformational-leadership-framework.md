# TRANSFORMATIONAL LEADERSHIP FRAMEWORK GUIDE
## Outcome-Measure-Coach Examples & Templates
* * *
## THE FRAMEWORK OVERVIEW
**OUTCOME** → Define crystal-clear success criteria **MEASURE** → Put metric on scorecard with their name
**COACH** → Address issues in weekly 1-on-1s, not real-time
**The Problem This Solves:** Every time you jump in to solve their problems, you teach them to wait for you to rescue them. This framework builds capable leaders who solve problems without you.
* * *
## COMPLETE EXAMPLE: IMPROVING CUSTOMER ONBOARDING
### STEP 1: OUTCOME
**Bad outcome (vague):** "Improve customer onboarding."
**Good outcome (crystal-clear):**

```cs
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PROJECT: Customer Onboarding Improvement
OWNER: James Park (Customer Success Manager)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:
When this project is successful, we will see:
• 80% of new customers complete onboarding within 7 days (up from current 65%)
• Average time-to-first-value reduced from 9 days to 5 days
• Zero customers churning during first 30 days due to setup confusion

DEFINITION OF DONE:
• Onboarding completion rate hits 80% for 4 consecutive weeks
• New onboarding process fully documented with screenshots
• Support tickets related to setup reduced by 50%
• Timeline: March 15, 2026

WHAT SUCCESS MEANS FOR THE TEAM:
✅ POSITIVE: If we hit this target, we'll reduce early-stage churn by approximately 40%, which means $15k/month in saved MRR. That gives us budget to hire another CS team member in Q2.

❌ NEGATIVE: If we don't fix this, we'll continue losing 8-10 customers per month during onboarding, which puts our growth targets at risk and delays the team expansion we planned.

YOUR AUTHORITY:
You can:
• Redesign the onboarding email sequence
• Create new tutorial content
• Schedule live onboarding sessions
• Reallocate CS team time to support this

You need approval for:
• Hiring contractors or agencies
• Spending >$2,000 on tools
• Major product changes (work with Priya)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

**Conversation to set this outcome:**

```dart
MANAGER: "James, I want to talk about our onboarding problem. Walk me through what you see."

JAMES: "We're losing too many customers in the first month. They sign up, get confused, and leave."

MANAGER: "What does success look like to you? If this was completely fixed, what would be different?"

JAMES: "More people would actually complete setup and start using the product."

MANAGER: "Be specific. What percentage? In what timeframe?"

JAMES: "I'd say 80% completing within a week, versus the 65% we have now."

MANAGER: "Great. And what's the business impact of hitting that?"

JAMES: "We'd stop losing those 8-10 customers per month who churn because they never got set up properly."

MANAGER: "Which is about $15k in MRR we're currently bleeding. Okay, here's your outcome..."

[Reviews the written outcome document together]

MANAGER: "Does this clearly define what you're going after?"

JAMES: "Yes, crystal clear."

MANAGER: "Do you believe you can hit this by March 15?"

JAMES: "Yes, if I can reallocate some CS time to build better onboarding."

MANAGER: "You have that authority. Let's talk about how we'll measure this."
```

* * *
### STEP 2: MEASURE
**Metric added to scorecard:**

```gherkin
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER SUCCESS METRICS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

METRIC                      | DRI         | TARGET | ACTUAL | STATUS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Onboarding Completion % (7d)| James Park  | 80%    | 68%    | 🟡
Time-to-First-Value (days)  | James Park  | 5      | 8.2    | 🟡
First-30-Day Churn %        | James Park  | 0%     | 2.8%   | 🔴

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

**Why this works:**
✅ James's name is next to the metric (he owns it)
✅ Everyone on the team can see his progress
✅ The target is clear (80%, not "better")
✅ Color-coding creates natural accountability
✅ He'll self-correct without needing to be told
**Conversation setting up measurement:**

```bash
MANAGER: "Now we need to put this on the scorecard. What's the main metric that tells us if you're succeeding?"

JAMES: "Onboarding completion rate within 7 days."

MANAGER: "Exactly. That's going on the scorecard with your name next to it. Target is 80%. Every week in our sync meeting, you'll report this number. Green means you're at 80%+, yellow means 70-79%, red means below 70%. Sound fair?"

JAMES: "Yep, makes sense."

MANAGER: "Here's the key: your name is on this number. The whole team will see it every week. When it's red, they'll see it. When it's green, they'll see it. This creates healthy accountability—you'll feel the weight of that number without me having to nag you about it."

JAMES: "I get it. I'll be motivated to move it."

MANAGER: "Exactly. That's the point. You'll problem-solve on your own to make that number green because YOU own it. And if you get stuck, we'll address it in our weekly 1-on-1. Make sense?"

JAMES: "Totally clear."
```

* * *
### STEP 3: COACH
**Two weeks later—James's metric is still yellow/red. Time for coaching in the 1-on-1.**
**1-on-1 Coaching Conversation:**

```bash
MANAGER: "Hey James, let's talk about onboarding completion. You're at 68%, target is 80%. Walk me through what's happening."

JAMES: "I've been working on the email sequence redesign, but it's taking longer than expected. I'm also trying to build tutorial videos, but I don't have video editing skills."

MANAGER: "Okay, so you're working on it, but you're stuck on execution. Let me share a story. When I was in your role at my last company, I tried to do everything myself—redesign emails, create videos, update documentation—and I got overwhelmed. Nothing got done well because I was spread too thin."

JAMES: "Yeah, that's exactly how I feel."

MANAGER: "Here's what I learned: the principle is focus on the highest-leverage activity first. Not everything matters equally. If you could only do ONE thing to improve onboarding completion, what would move the needle most?"

JAMES: "Honestly? Live onboarding sessions. When I personally walk someone through setup, they complete it 100% of the time. But I can't scale that."

MANAGER: "Can you scale it to twice a day? Two 30-minute group sessions?"

JAMES: "Yeah, I could do that."

MANAGER: "So instead of trying to do emails, videos, and docs all at once, what if you just launched live onboarding sessions this week and postponed the other stuff?"

JAMES: "That would definitely move the number faster."

MANAGER: "The principle here is: Do the thing that works, even if it doesn't scale perfectly yet. You can refine it later. Right now, you need to prove you can move that metric. What's your commitment?"

JAMES: "I'll launch live onboarding sessions starting Monday. Two sessions per day, 30 minutes each. I'll track completion rates and report back next week."

MANAGER: "Perfect. And if you're still below 75% next week, what will you try?"

JAMES: "I'll adjust the session timing or add a third session. I'll figure it out."

MANAGER: "Good. That's ownership. I believe you can get this to green. Let me know if you need anything from me."
```

**What just happened:**
✅ **Observed the issue** (metric still yellow/red)
✅ **Asked him to explain** (what's blocking you?)
✅ **Shared a personal story** (when I faced this...)
✅ **Taught the principle** (focus on highest-leverage activity first)
✅ **Got his solution** (he decided to do live sessions)
✅ **Confirmed commitment** (what will you do differently?)
**What did NOT happen:**
❌ Manager didn't jump in and solve it
❌ Manager didn't micromanage the approach
❌ Manager didn't tell him exactly what to do
❌ Manager didn't take over the work

## EXAMPLE 2: IMPROVING SALES CONVERSION RATE
### STEP 1: OUTCOME

```erlang
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PROJECT: Increase Demo-to-Close Conversion Rate
OWNER: Sarah Chen (Sales Manager)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:
• Demo-to-close conversion rate increases from 32% to 45%
• Average sales cycle shortens from 25 days to 18 days
• Sales team closes 10+ deals per month consistently (vs current 6-7)

DEFINITION OF DONE:
• Conversion rate hits 45% for 8 consecutive weeks
• New sales process documented and adopted by entire team
• Sales team trained on new objection handling techniques
• Timeline: April 30, 2026

WHAT SUCCESS MEANS:
✅ POSITIVE: Hitting 45% conversion means we'll hit our $60k MRR target by end of Q2, which unlocks budget for the marketing expansion we've been planning.

❌ NEGATIVE: Staying at 32% means we'll miss revenue targets and have to delay hiring the content marketer and paid ads specialist.

YOUR AUTHORITY:
• Redesign sales process and demo structure
• Implement new CRM workflows
• Train sales team on new techniques
• Adjust pricing/packaging presentation

Need approval for:
• Changing actual pricing
• Major discounting (>15%)
• Hiring sales contractors

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

* * *
### STEP 2: MEASURE

```gherkin
SALES METRICS                  | DRI         | TARGET | ACTUAL | STATUS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Demos Booked (weekly)          | Sarah Chen  | 25     | 27     | 🟢
Demo-to-Close Conversion %     | Sarah Chen  | 45%    | 35%    | 🟡
Avg Sales Cycle (days)         | Sarah Chen  | 18     | 23     | 🟡
Deals Closed (monthly)         | Sarah Chen  | 10     | 7      | 🔴
```

**Public accountability:** Sarah's name is on these metrics. The whole team sees them every week.
* * *
### STEP 3: COACH
**Coaching Conversation (1-on-1 after 3 weeks):**

```bash
MANAGER: "Sarah, your conversion is at 35%, up from 32% but still below the 45% target. What's working and what's not?"

SARAH: "The new demo structure is helping—people are more engaged. But I'm losing deals at the pricing conversation. They love the product but balk at the cost."

MANAGER: "Okay, so demos are better, but pricing objections are killing deals. Let me ask you: are these legitimate budget concerns or are they using price as an excuse?"

SARAH: "Honestly? I think it's an excuse. When I dig deeper, it's usually that they don't fully believe in the ROI yet."

MANAGER: "That's a great insight. So it's not actually a pricing problem—it's a value communication problem."

SARAH: "Yeah, I think so."

MANAGER: "I learned this the hard way early in my sales career. I kept trying to discount my way to closes, but that didn't work. Here's the principle: Price objections are almost never about price—they're about perceived value. If someone truly believes they'll get 10x ROI, price becomes irrelevant."

SARAH: "That makes sense."

MANAGER: "So how could you strengthen your value presentation during demos?"

SARAH: "I could spend more time on the ROI calculator, show them specific numbers based on their business."

MANAGER: "Exactly. And what about case studies? Do you show similar companies who've gotten results?"

SARAH: "I could do that better. Right now I just mention them, but I don't show the actual data."

MANAGER: "So here's your commitment: For the next two weeks, every demo includes a customized ROI calculation and at least one relevant case study with specific results. Deal?"

SARAH: "Deal. I'll track how many pricing objections I get after implementing this."

MANAGER: "Perfect. If you're still below 40% conversion in two weeks, we'll troubleshoot further. But I think this will move the needle. Sound good?"

SARAH: "Yep, I'm on it."
```

## EXAMPLE 3: REDUCING CUSTOMER SUPPORT TICKET VOLUME
### STEP 1: OUTCOME

```sql
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PROJECT: Reduce Support Ticket Volume Through Better Self-Service
OWNER: Priya Kumar (Product Manager)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:
• Support ticket volume decreases from 250/week to 150/week
• 70% of common issues resolved via help center (vs current 40%)
• Average ticket resolution time drops from 8 hours to 4 hours
• Customer satisfaction with support remains 8.5+ out of 10

DEFINITION OF DONE:
• Help center articles cover the top 20 support issues (currently only 8)
• Video tutorials created for 10 most common problems
• In-app help widget implemented with contextual suggestions
• Ticket volume sustained below 160/week for 6 consecutive weeks
• Timeline: May 15, 2026

WHAT SUCCESS MEANS:
✅ POSITIVE: Reducing ticket volume by 40% frees up support team capacity to focus on high-value customer engagement instead of repetitive questions. We can reallocate 20 hours/week to proactive outreach.

❌ NEGATIVE: If ticket volume stays high, we'll need to hire another support person ($50k/year) instead of investing that budget in product improvements.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

* * *
### STEP 2: MEASURE

```gherkin
CUSTOMER SUPPORT METRICS        | DRI         | TARGET | ACTUAL | STATUS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Support Tickets (weekly)        | Priya Kumar | 150    | 215    | 🔴
Self-Service Resolution %       | Priya Kumar | 70%    | 48%    | 🟡
Avg Ticket Resolution (hours)   | Priya Kumar | 4      | 7.2    | 🟡
Customer Satisfaction Score     | Priya Kumar | 8.5    | 8.6    | 🟢
```

* * *
### STEP 3: COACH
**Coaching Conversation:**

```less
MANAGER: "Priya, ticket volume is still at 215 versus your 150 target. You're stuck in red. What's the blocker?"

PRIYA: "I've been working on help articles, but I'm only averaging 2 per week. At this rate, it'll take 6 weeks to cover the top 20 issues."

MANAGER: "Why only 2 per week? What's taking so long?"

PRIYA: "I'm trying to make them perfect—detailed screenshots, multiple examples, comprehensive FAQs. Each one takes like 6-8 hours."

MANAGER: "Ah, I see the problem. You're aiming for perfection when you need speed. Let me tell you what I learned when I was building a knowledge base at my last company. I spent weeks creating these beautiful, comprehensive articles. Know what happened?"

PRIYA: "What?"

MANAGER: "Customers didn't read them. They were too long. They wanted quick answers, not comprehensive guides."

PRIYA: "Interesting."

MANAGER: "The principle here is: Done is better than perfect. A simple 300-word article with one screenshot that solves the problem is infinitely more valuable than a 2,000-word masterpiece that doesn't exist yet."

PRIYA: "So I should simplify them?"

MANAGER: "Exactly. What if you aimed for 5 simple articles per week instead of 2 perfect ones? You'd cover the top 20 issues in a month instead of three months."

PRIYA: "I could do that. Keep it to: What's the problem, what's the solution, one screenshot."

MANAGER: "Perfect. That's your new approach. And here's your commitment: 5 help articles this week covering the next 5 top issues. They don't need to be perfect. They need to exist. Deal?"

PRIYA: "Deal. I'll knock out 5 this week."

MANAGER: "And if ticket volume doesn't start dropping in two weeks, what will you try next?"

PRIYA: "I'll add the in-app help widget so people find the articles easier."

MANAGER: "Good thinking. I'm confident you'll get this to green. Let me know if you need anything."
```

## COACHING CONVERSATION TEMPLATE
**Use this structure for every coaching conversation:**

```cs
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1-ON-1 COACHING TEMPLATE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

STEP 1: REVIEW THE METRIC
"[Name], let's talk about [metric]. You're at [actual], target is [target]. Walk me through what's happening."

[Let them explain—don't interrupt]

STEP 2: DIAGNOSE THE BLOCKER
"So it sounds like the issue is [reflect back what they said]. Is that accurate?"

[Confirm understanding]

"What specifically is getting in the way of hitting the target?"

[Let them identify the blocker]

STEP 3: SHARE YOUR STORY
"Let me tell you about a time I faced something similar..."

[Share a relevant personal story of how you overcame a similar challenge]

"Here's what I learned: [lesson/principle]"

STEP 4: TEACH THE PRINCIPLE
"The key principle here is: [underlying concept, not specific task]"

Examples:
• "Focus on the highest-leverage activity first"
• "Done is better than perfect"
• "Price objections are value objections"
• "Speed beats perfection when you're learning"
• "You can't optimize what doesn't exist yet"

STEP 5: GET THEIR SOLUTION
"So given that principle, how do you think you should approach this differently?"

[Let them come up with the solution]

"What specifically will you do this week?"

[Get concrete commitment]

STEP 6: CONFIRM COMMITMENT
"Great. So your commitment is [repeat their commitment]. Is that right?"

[Get verbal confirmation]

"And if you're still below [threshold] next week, what will you try?"

[Get backup plan]

STEP 7: OFFER SUPPORT
"What support do you need from me to make this happen?"

[Listen and provide if reasonable]

"I believe you can get this to green. Let me know how it goes."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## WHAT TO COACH VS. WHAT TO FIX YOURSELF
### COACH THEM WHEN:
✅ It's a skill they need to develop
✅ The solution requires their domain expertise
✅ They have the authority to solve it
✅ It's a learning opportunity
✅ They'll face this situation again
**Example:** Sales rep struggling with objection handling → Coach them on the principles of value-based selling
* * *
### FIX IT YOURSELF WHEN:
❌ It's a systems/tools problem outside their control
❌ It requires budget approval or strategic decision
❌ It's a one-time crisis that needs immediate action
❌ They genuinely lack the authority to solve it
❌ The business can't afford the learning time
**Example:** CRM system is broken and they can't log deals → You fix the system, don't coach them to "figure it out"
* * *
## RED FLAGS YOU'RE MICROMANAGING
🚩 You're telling them exactly HOW to do their work
🚩 You're solving their problems daily instead of weekly coaching
🚩 You're jumping into their meetings or calls uninvited
🚩 You're redoing their work because it's not "your way"
🚩 You're making their decisions for them
🚩 They wait for you to tell them what to do next
🚩 You feel like you can't take a day off without things breaking
**Remember:** Every time you rescue them, you train them to wait for rescue.
* * *
## PRINCIPLES TO TEACH (NOT TASKS)
**Bad coaching (task-focused):** "You need to send the follow-up email within 24 hours and include these three bullet points."
**Good coaching (principle-focused):** "The principle is: Strike while the iron is hot. Leads go cold fast. What's your plan to stay top-of-mind with prospects?"
* * *
**Bad coaching (task-focused):** "Use this exact script when handling pricing objections."
**Good coaching (principle-focused):** "The principle is: Price objections are almost never about price—they're about perceived value. How could you strengthen your value presentation?"
* * *
**Bad coaching (task-focused):** "Write help articles in this specific format with these exact sections."
**Good coaching (principle-focused):** "The principle is: Done is better than perfect when you're solving urgent problems. What's the fastest way to get useful content in front of customers?"
* * *
## COMMON COACHING MISTAKES TO AVOID
❌ **Mistake 1: Coaching in real-time when you see problems**
*   This is micromanaging, not coaching
*   Write down the issue, address it in the weekly 1-on-1
❌ **Mistake 2: Telling them what to do instead of asking**
*   "Here's what you should do..." → You're solving it for them
*   "What do you think you should do?" → They develop problem-solving skills
❌ **Mistake 3: Not getting commitment to change behavior**
*   Coaching without commitment = wasted time
*   Always end with: "What's your commitment going forward?"
❌ **Mistake 4: Coaching on everything at once**
*   Pick 1-2 most important issues per 1-on-1
*   Overloading them creates overwhelm and inaction
❌ **Mistake 5: Not following up on commitments**
*   If they commit to something and don't do it, address it
*   No follow-up = no accountability = broken trust
* * *
## OUTCOME-MEASURE-COACH SUCCESS CHECKLIST
**For each person on your team, can you answer YES to these?**
□ They have a crystal-clear outcome they're working toward
□ There's a specific metric on the scorecard with their name on it
□ The metric is public—everyone can see their progress
□ We have weekly 1-on-1s where I coach them (not solve for them)
□ When I see problems, I write them down for the 1-on-1
□ I teach principles, not specific tasks
□ I get commitments to behavioral change
□ They're problem-solving without waiting for me
□ I'm NOT jumping in to rescue them daily
□ Trust is high—they know I support them but won't do their job
**If you answered NO to any of these, that's your coaching opportunity.**
