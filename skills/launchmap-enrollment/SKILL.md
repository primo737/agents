## What Is the Enrollment Script?

This is the framework for a consultative sales conversation — not a pitch, a prescription. The client tells you what they need. You prescribe the solution. You don't sell; you enroll.

The structure is 6 phases. Each phase has a checkpoint. You don't move forward until the checkpoint is passed.

---

## Phase 1: FRAME — Set the Context

Before any discovery, set the container for the call.

**3-Part Frame:**

1. **Time:** "We have [X] minutes together. Is that still good for you?"

2. **Agenda:** "Here's what I'd like to do — I'll ask you some questions to understand where you are, and if I think I can help you, I'll share what that looks like. If I can't help you, I'll tell you that too. Sound good?"

3. **Permission to be honest:** "The most valuable thing I can do for you today is be completely honest. So I'm going to ask you some real questions. Can I count on you to be honest with me too?"

**Checkpoint:** Did they agree to all three? If yes, proceed. If they're resistant, this may not be the right prospect.

---

## Phase 2: DISCOVER — Find the Numbers

Two questions that reveal everything:

**Question 1: Where are you now?**
Get specific numbers. Revenue, clients, hours, whatever the relevant metric is for this avatar.
"Walk me through where your business/life is right now. Give me the real numbers."

**Question 2: Where do you want to be?**
In 12 months. In 3 years. Specific numbers.
"If everything went exactly how you wanted — where would you be in the next 12 months? Give me numbers."

**The Gap:** Note the distance between Q1 and Q2. This is the gap your program fills.

**Product Gap Audit:** 
Ask what they've already tried. Not to judge — to understand. What didn't work about it? What was missing?

---

## Phase 3: PROBLEMS — Surface the Cost

Don't rush here. The cost of inaction is the most powerful motivator.

**Struggles:** "What's the biggest challenge right now that's stopping you from getting to [goal]?"

**Cost of inaction:** "If you stay where you are for another 12 months — what does that cost you? In money? In time? In opportunity? In stress?"

**Why now:** "Why is this important to solve *now* and not in 6 months?"

**Value Checkpoint:** Ask them to articulate back to you why this matters. "So if I heard you right, the reason you need to solve this now is ___. Is that right?" 

They should say yes enthusiastically. If they're lukewarm — probe more.

---

## Phase 4: PRESCRIPTION — Give Them the Plan

This is where you share your methodology. Not the offer yet — the thinking.

**Ask permission:** "Can I tell you what I'd recommend based on what you've shared?"

**3-Part Action Plan:** Share 3 specific actions they need to take to move from where they are to where they want to be. Make these real, make them specific, make them sequential. This is not the program — this is the strategy.

Action 1: [First thing they need to do]
Action 2: [Second thing]
Action 3: [Third thing]

**Confidence Checkpoint:** After sharing the plan, ask:
"On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that if you followed these three steps, you'd get to [goal]?"

**Required response: 5 or above.** If they say 4 or less — ask what would need to change for that number to go up. If they can't get to 5, this isn't the right fit.

If 5–7: "What would make it a 10?"
If 8–10: Perfect. Proceed.

---

## Phase 5: APPLICATION — Present the Transformation

This is NOT a feature pitch. Present the transformation, not the program.

Wrong: "The program includes 12 weekly calls, a private Facebook group, and 3 bonus trainings."
Right: "By the end of our work together, you'll have [specific result], you'll feel [specific feeling], and you'll no longer [specific pain]."

Then: Describe the 3 stages of the program (from the roadmap) as a journey:
- "In Stage 1, you'll [outcome]..."
- "In Stage 2, you'll [outcome]..."  
- "In Stage 3, you'll [outcome]..."

**Qualification:** Before sharing the price, qualify:
"I want to make sure this is the right fit on both sides. The people I work with are typically [avatar description]. Does that sound like you?"

If yes: proceed.
If no: be honest. "Based on what you've shared, I'm not sure this is the right fit right now because ___. Here's what I'd recommend instead."

---

## Phase 6: INVITATION — Make the Offer

**State the price clearly. Then stop talking.**

"To work with me over [duration], the investment is [price]."

Then: silence. Wait. Let them respond.

(Most sales are lost because the seller talks after naming the price.)

**Handle objections with stories, not arguments:**

For every common objection, have a story ready:
- "I need to think about it" → story of someone who waited and what it cost them
- "I can't afford it" → story of someone who couldn't afford NOT to do it
- "I need to talk to my spouse" → story of someone who did that and what happened

**Wins Matrix:** Before wrapping up, share 2–3 specific client results relevant to this prospect's situation. Not testimonials — mini-stories. "Someone very similar to you came in at [their starting point] and after [program], they [specific result]."
