## What Is Traffic On Demand?

Organic content builds authority. Paid traffic builds the list. Both work — but paid traffic gives you control. You decide when the faucet opens and at what rate.

This step builds the cold traffic strategy — reaching people who don't know you yet and getting them to take the first step.

The goal of cold traffic ads: **get the opt-in.** Not the sale. Not the call. The opt-in.

## Exercise 1: Hot Currencies

The avatar has currencies they'll spend money and attention on. Paid ads must speak to those currencies immediately.

Ask: "What does your avatar want so badly they'd stop whatever they're doing to get it? Think about the emotion, not the product."

Review currencies identified in the MDM step:
- Time
- Money
- Relationships
- Health
- Status
- Freedom
- Security
- Impact

**Primary currency for ads:** [Identify the most emotionally charged one]

All ad hooks and headlines will tap this currency.

## Exercise 2: Ad Hooks and Angles

A hook is the first line — it determines whether they keep reading. An angle is the frame or perspective.

**5 Core Angle Types:**

**1. Case Study** — Results-first. "How [person like avatar] [achieved result] in [timeframe]."
Best for: warm audiences, retargeting, social proof campaigns.

**2. Template/Checklist** — Give them something. "Download the exact [resource] we used to [result]."
Best for: cold traffic, lead magnet campaigns, top-of-funnel.

**3. Comparison** — Contrast old vs. new. "Stop doing [wrong thing]. Do [right thing] instead."
Best for: awareness stage, problem-aware audiences.

**4. Question** — Lead with curiosity. "Are you making this [specific mistake] when [doing thing]?"
Best for: scroll-stopping, pattern-interrupt, cold traffic.

**5. How-To** — Direct value promise. "How to [achieve specific result] without [pain]."
Best for: search intent audiences, YouTube pre-roll, content ads.

Ask: "Which of these angles fits your avatar's current awareness level? Are they problem-aware, solution-aware, or product-aware?"

Build 2–3 hooks for each angle type selected.

## Exercise 3: Ad Concepts

For each lead magnet, build 2–3 complete ad concepts.

**Headline Rules:**
- Start with an action verb ("Download," "Get," "Discover," "Stop," "Try")
- Include the result
- Under 10 words ideally
- Never start with "Are you a [label]"

**Body Copy Formula (5 sentences):**
1. Hook / Pattern interrupt
2. Identify the problem (in their words)
3. Introduce the solution (what this resource does)
4. Proof (one result, specific)
5. CTA (one action, clear)

**Creative Direction:**
> Use an image of the lead magnet itself — the checklist, the template, the quiz cover page. This IS the creative. No stock photos of smiling people. The resource image signals value before they read a word.

Walk through building 2 complete ad concepts for the core ULM.

---

## Exercise 4: Testing Plan

Structured testing prevents wasted spend. Build a 3-layer test:

**Layer 1: Audience test** (Week 1–2)
Test 3–4 audience segments. Same ad, different audiences.
- Lookalike from email list
- Interest-based (competitors' pages, relevant topics)
- Broad (age/gender/location only)
- Behavioral (engaged shoppers, active page fans)

Budget: $5–10/day per audience. Kill anything with CPM > $30 after 3 days.

**Layer 2: Creative test** (Week 3–4)
Once winning audience found, test 3–4 ad creatives against each other.
- Static image of lead magnet
- Video testimonial
- Carousel with checklist items
- Quote graphic

Budget: $10–15/day. Optimize for opt-in cost (CPL < $5 target for most markets).

**Layer 3: Hook test** (Week 5+)
Same creative, test 3 different headlines/first lines.
- Question hook
- Statement hook
- How-to hook

Scale winner. Kill losers.

Ask: "What's your starting ad budget? And what platform are you launching on first?"
