## What Makes a Lead Magnet "Ultimate"

A lead magnet is not bait. It's the first act of generosity in a relationship.

The ULM must:
1. Be immediately valuable (they use it now, not someday)
2. Solve ONE specific micro-problem (not five problems)
3. Create an "aha" that makes them want the full solution
4. Be connected to your core offer (the ULM gives the WHAT, the offer gives the HOW)

The 5-second test: Can someone look at your lead magnet and immediately know (a) who it's for, (b) what they get, (c) what they do with it?

---

## Exercise 1: Temperature Check

Traffic arrives at different temperatures. The ULM must match where the audience is.

**HOT traffic** — they know you, trust you, are already interested. A simple opt-in works.
**WARM traffic** — they know the problem exists, don't know you yet. Need credibility baked in.
**COLD traffic** — problem-unaware. The ULM must first create awareness before offering value.

Ask: "Where is most of your current traffic coming from — warm referrals, cold paid ads, or organic content? Rate your audience temperature: HOT, WARM, or COLD."

Based on their answer:
- HOT → lean checklist, template, or quick-win resource
- WARM → quiz, assessment, or value-packed guide
- COLD → quiz or awareness-first content (paradigm shift, not product pitch)

## Exercise 2: Core ULM Design

Walk through these choices:

**Format options:**
- ✅ Checklist (easiest to consume, highest perceived value per word)
- ✅ Swipe file / template (immediately usable)
- ✅ Quiz / Assessment (interactive, personalized results)
- ✅ Mini-training / Video (great for warm audiences)
- ✅ Calculator / Tool (solves a specific numerical problem)
- ⚠️ Ebook / Long guide (low completion rates — avoid unless specific reason)

> Recommendation: Start with a checklist. The image of the checklist IS the ad creative — you save money on design because the lead magnet IS the visual.

Ask: "What's one specific problem your avatar faces *right before* they'd be ready to buy your core offer? Something they can solve in under 20 minutes with the right guidance?"

Design the ULM around that micro-problem.

**ULM Naming Formula:**
`[Number] [Adjective] [Noun] to [Specific Result] [Without/In] [Pain/Timeframe]`

Examples:
- "7 Questions to Ask Before Hiring Your First VA (Without Losing Money on a Bad Hire)"
- "The 5-Minute Morning Audit Checklist for Founders Who Skip Workouts"

Draft 3 title options. Choose the one that would make the avatar stop scrolling.

---

## Exercise 3: Step-Specific Lead Magnets

The 9-step product roadmap creates natural lead magnet opportunities. Pick 2–3 steps that have a specific, demonstrable micro-problem and build a ULM for each.

For each selected step:
- What's the micro-problem at this step?
- What checklist, template, or resource would solve it?
- Who specifically does this attract? (early stage vs. advanced?)

These become your lead magnet library — different ads can test different ULMs with the same audience.
