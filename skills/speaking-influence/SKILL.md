# Speaking Influence: Craft Stories That Change Minds

**Source:** Eric Edmeades — Speaking & Influence Mastery (Craft Stories That Change Minds, Parts 1 & 2 + Anatomy of a Magnetic Story)
**Prerequisite skill:** magnetic-story

---

## The Five Levels of Impact

Every story you tell operates at one of these levels. Stack them deliberately.

1. **Entertain** — Make them feel. Non-negotiable minimum. Without this, nothing else lands.
2. **Inspire** — Give hope and possibility. Show it's achievable. Triggers mirror neurons — when the audience admires you, they unconsciously model your behavior.
3. **Teach** — Create a memory of something new. Use story, not lecture. The goal is a memory of having done it, not knowing about it.
4. **Paradigm Shift** — Change how they think about something. Meet them where they are, align, then redirect. Use the Aikido principle.
5. **Inception** — They arrive at a new realization that feels like *their own idea*. Black-belt level. The moment you try to sell, it stops working.

**Test for Inception:** They say "I've always wanted that" about something they only recently learned about.

---

## The Aikido Principle (for Paradigm Shifts)

Aikido is a defensive martial art — align with the attacker's energy, then redirect it. Apply this to belief change:

1. Find every single thing you can agree with in their current position
2. Never use "yes, but" — find genuine alignment first
3. Redirect naturally from shared ground

**Feel, Felt, Found frame:**
- "I know how you *feel* about that..."
- "I *felt* that way before..."
- "And then I *found*..."

**Rule:** You should not enter a debate on any topic unless you could confidently argue *either side*.

## The 4-Step Inceptive Process

1. **Who am I trying to reach?** Think broader — not just people who want your thing, but people who don't yet know they need it.
2. **Where are they now, and where do I want to take them?** Be specific about the transformation.
3. **Experience-driven or data-driven approach?** Choose based on audience velocity and emotional state.
4. **Tell the story to awaken the desire, THEN teach.** Never sell. Let them come to you with buying questions.

**Your inceptive story is often your own story of arriving at the conclusion you want them to reach.**

## Emotional Anchoring

Your audience is always unconsciously anchoring emotional states to your face and voice. It's not *whether* they anchor — it's *which* emotions they anchor.

- Pick 1–2 emotions you want as your brand
- Build peak moments of those specific emotions into every story
- Use facial expressions, vocal variation, and physical presence deliberately — these carry 70%+ of story impact

## The 8 Story Mechanics (Execution Checklist)

1. **Sensory details** — ground the audience in the scene (especially smell)
2. **Strategic pauses** — create cognitive space; most underused tool in storytelling
3. **Emotional peaks** — trigger mirror neurons; feel it yourself as you tell it
4. **Volume & vocal variation** — tone carries as much as words
5. **Facial expressions** — non-verbal authenticity
6. **Body language** — physical presence and conviction
7. **Self-talk / internal monologue** — "I said to myself..." shows thinking, not just action
8. **Authentic vulnerability** — cannot be performed; must be accessed

**Key principle:** "Reporting facts vs. storytelling" — the difference is entirely in these mechanics.

## The Concrete Example Mandate (non-negotiable)

Every concept, every term, every abstraction in a talk MUST land with a concrete, relatable example a non-expert instantly pictures. No exceptions.

**Why:** People do not remember definitions. They remember pictures. A concept without a picture is a concept that did not transfer. If they cannot picture it, they did not learn it.

**How to apply:**
- For every term, ask: *what is this LIKE in their everyday life?* Then lead with that, not the term.
- Use kitchens, restaurants, houses, traffic, cars, post offices, light switches, hotel keys — things that exist in the audience's body, not their browser.
- The pattern is **picture first, label second.** "Imagine a waiter who runs to the kitchen for you. That waiter is what we call an API." NOT "An API, or Application Programming Interface, is..."
- One example per concept minimum. If the concept has two parts, use two examples.
- If you catch yourself defining a word with another word the audience also does not know, stop. Build the picture first.
- Test: read your draft as if you were a smart 12-year-old. If a 12-year-old loses the thread, the example is missing or weak.

**Avoid:** dictionary definitions, jargon stacked on jargon, "in technical terms," "essentially," and any sentence that requires the listener to already know the field.

## Influence Architecture Summary

```
Audience enters → Meet them at their belief
                ↓
        Align (Aikido) — never argue
                ↓
   Tell story from your own realization
                ↓
     They experience the contradiction
                ↓
    They resolve it themselves (inception)
                ↓
  They ask the buying question. You didn't sell.
```
