# Digital Marketing Onboarding

Build a usable marketing strategy from the client's actual business, audience, evidence, and delivery capacity. This skill is a guided discovery and production workflow. It can produce a full onboarding package or one requested component. Do not reduce a full onboarding to a generic checklist, and do not make a focused request wait for every other component.

## Start by setting the scope

1. Identify the deliverable: an interview, a particular module, a complete strategy, or implementation of approved work. Identify the intended audience for the output separately from the person or agent who will execute it.
2. Read the supplied brief, files, and conversation before asking questions. Preserve agreed goals, constraints, decisions, language, brand, and unfinished requirements across turns. A correction replaces only the item corrected.
3. Create a working record with five labels: **confirmed by client**, **verified from source**, **inference**, **illustrative proposal**, and **unknown or awaiting approval**. Record the source/date for consequential facts. Keep each client's records, accounts, credentials, creative, testimonials, and permissions separate.
4. For a full onboarding, use the eleven modules below as coverage areas and progress from discovery toward implementation. Reorder or combine modules when the business requires it; mark a module *complete*, *draft*, *not applicable*, or *blocked* with a reason. For a focused task, use only relevant modules and note dependencies that materially affect the answer.
5. Ask only for information that changes an important choice, claim, commitment, targeting decision, or external action. Batch related questions and offer a clearly labeled draft where lower-impact details are missing. If the user requests an interview, conduct it before presenting the final strategy. Do not manufacture missing facts to avoid asking.
6. Draft within the user's request. Execute within available capabilities and authorization already given. Obtain approval for a live action only when it has not been authorized; verify anything saved, sent, published, or changed at its destination.

## 1. Clarity call and intake

**Purpose:** Establish the business reality and the decision this marketing work must improve.

Use the [intake question bank](references/onboarding-questions.md) conversationally. Cover business model, current and intended customers, offer and price, differentiation, goal and timeframe, existing channels and baseline, sales and delivery process, proof, constraints, and decision owners. Do not make all fifteen questions mandatory if answers are already known or irrelevant. For a full interview, move through business, metrics, customers, and desires in manageable batches.

Produce a **brief of record**: confirmed facts, current baseline, desired outcome, audience hypotheses, offer and delivery boundaries, source list, approvals, and open decisions. Record the [core content fields](references/core-content-template.md) only as far as evidence supports them. A timeframe or result is a claim to substantiate, not an empty slot to fill.

If work includes recurring client delivery, community/events, or sensitive services, use only the relevant parts of the [conditional operations supplement](references/client-operations-community-psychosocial-intake.md). Identify the service owner, communications authority, escalation route, and data boundaries before proposing operational handoffs.

## 2. Market research and audience profiles

**Purpose:** Understand who buys, who uses, who influences, and what each needs to decide.

Review available interviews, sales notes, support questions, analytics, market material, and competitor positioning. Note what each source can and cannot establish. Describe the buyer first; add user, approver, partner/amplifier, or attention audience only when their roles change the strategy. For each useful profile, capture goal, trigger, obstacle, objection, decision criterion, information source, channel behavior, and evidence. Use the [audience guide](references/customer-avatar-guide.md).

Deliver one or more **evidence-labeled audience profiles** and a short list of claims to validate. Do not invent quotes or default to age, gender, income, marital status, or other demographics. Check any targeting restrictions before turning an attribute into an ad audience.

## 3. Customer journey

**Purpose:** Show how a person moves from first contact through a useful outcome and what can prevent the next step.

Map the actual or proposed stages: discovery, evaluation, enquiry or registration, decision, purchase or approval, onboarding, use, retention, and advocacy where applicable. The eight-stage Awareness → Engagement → Subscription → Conversion → Excitement → Ascension → Advocacy → Promotion model is available when useful; do not require a subscription, ascension, or promotion stage where none fits.

For each relevant stage record the customer's question, current/proposed touchpoint, message or asset, next action, owner, transition trigger, and measure. Mark breaks in the current journey and distinguish observed behavior from the proposed design. Deliver a **journey map with prioritized friction points**.

## 4. Positioning and statement of value

**Purpose:** Say what the offer does, for whom, and why this client can credibly deliver it.

Use the confirmed audience problem, approved offer, method, differentiation, and proof. Draft two or three distinct positioning options when there is a real choice, then explain the tradeoff. A useful starting shape is “For [audience facing problem], [offer] helps [supported outcome] through [method].” Include a result or timeframe only when supported. Compare the statement against actual service boundaries and the language customers use.

Deliver an **approved or reviewable positioning statement**, key message, supporting proof, and claim gaps. Do not turn a desired outcome into a guarantee.

## 5. Value ladder and core offer

**Purpose:** Make the real commercial path and delivery promise clear.

Document the approved core offer first: who it serves, what is included, price or pricing logic, eligibility, capacity, fulfilment, support, exclusions, and owner. If there are genuine entry, recurring, premium, or follow-on offers, map how a customer chooses or moves between them. A lead magnet, tripwire, upsell, down-sell, or value ladder is optional; fixed price bands do not determine the right path.

Deliver an **offer map** with known terms, proposed changes, dependencies, and decisions requiring approval. Keep proposed prices and guarantees visibly separate from approved terms.

## 6. Offer composition

**Purpose:** Explain the components of a multi-part offer without inflating its value.

If the offer has meaningful components, use the [offer composition guide](references/offer-stack-guide.md). Capture each component's function, customer benefit, delivery format, boundary, and evidence. Add a bonus only if it is real and useful. Compare a simple offer description against a component-based presentation; choose the clearer one.

Deliver a **customer-facing offer summary** plus an internal scope check. Do not invent component dollar values, savings, scarcity, bonuses, or regulatory assurances.

## 7. Funnel selection and mapping

**Purpose:** Give the target audience a coherent path to the approved next action.

Start with the goal: enquiry, booked call, purchase, registration, trial, renewal, or another meaningful action. Consider buying complexity, audience preference, traffic source, price and decision process, technology, staffing, and follow-up capacity. Use [funnel pattern selection](references/funnel-types-overview.md) and [adaptable structures](references/funnel-structures.md) as examples, not required architectures. Compare one or two viable paths if the choice is consequential.

For the selected path, map every retained touchpoint with: source → destination → page/message purpose → user action → data captured → owner → handoff → failure/recovery state → event measured. Note approvals and assets still needed. Deliver a **page-by-page or touchpoint-by-touchpoint funnel map** and explain why it fits this client. An opt-in, quiz, video, webinar, application, upsell, or retargeting step needs its own reason.

## 8. Lead magnet or entry resource, if useful

**Purpose:** Provide a valuable first step when education or qualification is needed before the core offer.

First decide whether a resource helps more than a direct enquiry, demo, event, or purchase path. If useful, identify one bounded audience problem and select an appropriate format: guide, checklist, tool, template, case study, workshop, assessment, or another format the client can deliver. Specify title, promise, outline, evidence, production owner, access method, and the next relevant action. If collecting contact details, state why and what follow-up is permitted.

Deliver a **resource brief and outline**, or record “not applicable” with a reason. Do not call an empty PDF a lead magnet or promise an unsupported result.

## 9. Follow-up and customer communications

**Purpose:** Help people complete the step they chose and support them after it.

Design only the sequences triggered by the actual path: enquiry response, resource delivery, event reminder, consideration/nurture, incomplete checkout, purchase confirmation, onboarding, support, renewal, or feedback. For each message, record audience/trigger, channel, timing, purpose, one key point, approved evidence, CTA, sender/owner, stop rule, and escalation. A table or compact message map can precede full copy.

Draft message copy in the current user's requested language and voice. Do not default to a fixed five-email sequence, cliffhangers, artificial urgency, or a specific SMS rule. Check channel permissions, opt-out handling, platform rules, and approved claims. Deliver a **sequence map and requested drafts**; test links, personalization, routing, and suppression before any live send.

## 10. Landing pages and conversion assets

**Purpose:** Make the offer understandable and the next step usable.

For each needed page, define audience, traffic source, page purpose, headline, supporting message, approved proof, offer terms, CTA, necessary form fields, privacy information, layout, mobile/accessibility needs, and confirmation/error state. Use the [landing-page review](references/landing-page-checklist.md) with *pass / change / unknown / not applicable*. Hero images, above-the-fold buttons, social sharing, and reduced navigation are design choices, not universal requirements.

Deliver a **page brief or copy/wireframe** and a test plan. Verify the full path from source through submission or payment to confirmation and handoff. Never use fabricated testimonials, logos, counts, or conversion statistics.

## 11. Measurement, learning, and retargeting

**Purpose:** Know whether the work achieves its goal and what to change next.

Define the primary outcome and supporting events, current baseline, calculation, source of truth, reporting cadence, owner, and decision rule. Map the minimum tracking needed: UTMs, analytics events, CRM stages, call attribution, or payment/booking events as applicable. Distinguish a lead, qualified enquiry, sale, delivered service, and attributable result.

If retargeting or customer-list use is proposed, check source and rights to the data, privacy/consent requirements, geography, current platform terms, account eligibility, sensitive-category restrictions, and client authority. Read [platform and claims checks](references/platform-and-claims.md). Do not upload a list merely because the client has it. If permissions or access are unresolved, leave that tactic out of the launch plan and identify an alternative.

Deliver a **measurement specification** and review cadence. Include retargeting audiences, creative, exclusions, budget, and suppression only when justified and permitted. Mark data limitations and attribution uncertainty.

## Final strategy document and handoff

For a full onboarding, compile the modules into one [strategy document](references/strategy-document-template.md). Lead with the decision: goal, audience, approved offer, recommended path, rationale, and first actions. Include evidence and assumptions, current baseline, customer journey, message and asset plan, delivery and approval owners, measurement, risks, and an ordered implementation backlog. Keep an open-decision log rather than hiding unresolved pricing, claims, permissions, legal/compliance review, or technical access.

Review the document for factual accuracy, requested scope, cross-client isolation, task compliance, feasibility, and operational handoff. Apply style and format preferences after those checks. Preserve justified uncertainty. A draft can be complete as a draft while live implementation remains unapproved or unverified; state each status plainly.
