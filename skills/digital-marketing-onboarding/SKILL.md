# Digital Marketing Onboarding

Establish what the business sells, who it serves, what it can prove, what outcome it wants, and what it can deliver. Turn those facts into the requested onboarding record or marketing plan. Use the current user's and workspace's preferences for language, brand, channels, and format. Keep different clients' facts, assets, accounts, and approvals separate.

## Execution contract

- Deliver the requested scope. A single funnel map or offer brief does not require eleven preliminary deliverables. A full onboarding request calls for an integrated strategy document.
- Reuse answers and decisions already supplied in this conversation or approved source files. Preserve agreed objectives and constraints across follow-ups; replace only what the user corrects.
- Ask for missing information only when it changes a consequential recommendation or action. Group questions by decision, use available evidence first, and state assumptions or options for lower-impact gaps. If an interview is explicitly requested, conduct it before compiling the final plan.
- Label client-supplied facts, independently verified evidence, illustrative examples, hypotheses, and unresolved decisions. Do not invent testimonials, results, prices, customer quotes, consent, or approval.
- Draft within the user's requested scope. Execute external actions only within available capabilities and the authorization already given; seek approval only for an action that actually lacks it. Verify any completed delivery or live change at its destination.

## Intake and strategy

Read [the intake questions](references/onboarding-questions.md) when discovery is needed. Ask only the questions relevant to the requested work. For a full intake, cover the business and offer, goals, current channels and baseline, purchase process, audience needs, proof, delivery capacity, and decision owners. Record unknowns and evidence sources. When ongoing client delivery, events/community, or sensitive services are in scope, use the [conditional operations supplement](references/client-operations-community-psychosocial-intake.md) only for the applicable domain.

Build the strategy from the evidence:

1. **Audience and buying context:** Define the buyer and any materially different influencer, partner, or end user. Capture goals, objections, decision criteria, relevant behaviors, channels, and source of evidence. Do not require demographic fields or infer protected traits.
2. **Positioning and offer:** State the audience, problem, supported outcome, method, scope, price or pricing logic if known, exclusions, and proof. Treat claims, guarantees, comparative assertions, and timeframes as unapproved until substantiated. Offer stacks and value ladders are optional tools, not default requirements.
3. **Journey and funnel:** Map the current or proposed path from discovery to decision, delivery, and retention. Show touchpoints, owner, handoff, next action, and success measure. Use a named funnel or eight-stage journey only when it helps the task; a lead magnet, upsell, or retargeting step is not inherently needed.
4. **Messages and assets:** Specify the landing page, lead magnet, email, SMS, event, or other assets actually needed. Match claims and calls to action to the approved offer and source. Use authentic proof only; mark illustrative copy clearly. Keep collection fields proportionate to the next step.
5. **Measurement and operation:** Define meaningful conversion events, baseline, reporting cadence, owner, and decision threshold. Check data rights, privacy/consent requirements, platform terms, geography, and account authority before proposing audience uploads, tracking, or retargeting. See [platform and claims checks](references/platform-and-claims.md).

## Deliverable and review

For a full onboarding, compile one strategy document with: confirmed facts and sources; audience and purchase context; goal and baseline; positioning and approved offer; recommended journey and assets; delivery capacity and owners; measurement plan; open decisions and next actions. For a narrower request, produce only the relevant sections. Include the reasoning for recommendations and distinguish alternatives where the choice is unresolved.

Before handing off, check factual accuracy, task coverage, client isolation, approvals and channel constraints, and feasibility. Then apply the user's style and format preferences. Preserve justified uncertainty instead of making the draft sound more certain than the evidence permits.
