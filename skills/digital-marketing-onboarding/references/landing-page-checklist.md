# Landing-page brief and review

Review the page against its actual goal, audience, source, device, and jurisdiction. Mark each check **pass**, **change**, **unknown**, or **not applicable**. No fixed count of design elements guarantees conversions.

## Review checks

1. **Arrival fit:** The page matches the search, referral, ad, email, or other source that brought the visitor. Audience and offer are recognizable without relying on jargon.
2. **Clarity:** The headline identifies the offer and next step. A quick comprehension check with an unfamiliar reader may help; there is no universal five-second threshold.
3. **Claim integrity:** Results, timeframes, comparisons, prices, scarcity, and guarantees are supported and approved. Testimonials, logos, ratings, and counts are authentic and permitted for this use.
4. **Action:** The CTA says what happens next, works on the relevant device, and leads to a usable form, checkout, scheduler, call, or other destination.
5. **Information architecture:** Navigation, imagery, social sharing, and visual cues serve the page's goal and the user's need for context. None is mandatory in every case.
6. **Data minimization:** Collect only fields needed for the stated next step. Present privacy information and obtain required permissions before tracking or follow-up.
7. **Accessibility and resilience:** Check keyboard access, readable contrast, labels, error messages, loading behavior, representative viewport sizes, and a fallback if the main action fails.
8. **End-to-end handoff:** Submit or purchase in a representative test. Confirm the promised response, routing, attribution, delivery, and follow-up actually occur.

A page brief is a draft until content and design are approved. A built page is not a verified funnel until its actual path and handoff have been tested. Record owner, date, and evidence for each live check.
