# Audience and decision-context guide

Build a profile only when it changes a marketing decision. Start with the buyer; add end user, approver, influencer, partner/amplifier, or attention audience when their goals or path differ. The number of profiles is determined by the business, not by a default of three. Separate observed evidence from a working hypothesis.

## Evidence to review

- Customer interviews, support conversations, sales calls, lost-deal reasons, on-site search, analytics, and existing CRM records, subject to access and privacy boundaries.
- Market and competitor material for alternative offers and messages. Competitor copy reveals positioning, not customer truth.
- The client's observations, labeled as client-supplied rather than independent research.

For each source, record date, sample or coverage, known bias, and the claim it supports. An isolated anecdote can generate a question; it does not establish a market-wide fact.

## Profile worksheet

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Role | Buyer, user, approver, influencer, referrer, or partner; relationship to the others |
| Situation and trigger | What changed, why the decision is being considered now |
| Desired progress | The outcome sought in their own verified terms if available |
| Current alternative | What they do instead and why it may be sufficient |
| Friction and objection | Cost, trust, time, fit, approval, implementation, or other evidenced barrier |
| Decision criteria | What they compare and who must sign off |
| Information path | Search, referral, community, event, seller, or other evidenced source |
| Channel and format | How they prefer to evaluate the offer, if known |
| Relevant context | Location, role, sector, business size, or other trait only when relevant and permitted |
| Evidence and confidence | Exact source, date, confirmed/inferred/unknown status |
| Marketing implication | A message, asset, offer, or funnel change this profile actually justifies |

Do not invent a quotation or pass a paraphrase off as customer voice. Keep demographic and sensitive traits out unless they are necessary, appropriate, and permitted for the intended use. Paid-ad targeting may have category and regional restrictions; see [platform and claims checks](platform-and-claims.md).

## Example, explicitly hypothetical

A scheduling-software team might hypothesize that the operations lead evaluates integrations while a finance approver evaluates recurring cost. That suggests testing two different FAQ sections. It is a research prompt, not a customer finding; verify it before using it as the basis for targeting or claims.

## Profile quality test

A profile is useful if it changes a real decision: which offer to show, what objection to answer, who approves, where to reach them, or what next action is appropriate. If two profiles lead to identical decisions, combine them. If a new role changes the buying path, add it. Mark gaps and propose the least burdensome validation method.
