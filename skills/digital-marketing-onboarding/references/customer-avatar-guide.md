# Audience and decision-context guide

Create only the profiles that improve the requested decision. A buyer, end user, approver, influencer, or partner may be distinct; three personas are not a requirement. Avoid filling a worksheet merely to make it look complete.

For each useful profile, record:

- Role in purchase, use, or referral; relationship to other decision-makers.
- Goal, trigger, obstacle, objection, and decision criterion.
- Relevant behavior, context, information source, and channel preference, with evidence.
- What is known, inferred, disputed, and still unverified.
- Implications for the offer, message, journey, or service delivery.

Use demographic or sensitive attributes only when relevant, appropriate, and permitted by applicable policy. Do not invent a “customer quote” to make a persona sound real. A hypothetical example must be labeled as hypothetical and never presented as research, testimony, or targeting evidence.

## Compact example

**Hypothetical B2B buyer:** An operations lead responsible for scheduling a distributed team. A possible decision criterion is whether existing calendar systems integrate. This is a hypothesis for interviews, not a finding about actual customers. Validate it against interviews, support logs, sales notes, or other permitted evidence before basing targeting or copy on it.
