# Customer Avatar Guide

A customer avatar (buyer persona) is the foundation of all marketing decisions. It
impacts content marketing, paid traffic, product creation, copywriting, and email
marketing. This guide walks through creating comprehensive avatars.

## The 5 Components of a Customer Avatar

### 1. Goals and Values

Goals and values relevant to the products/services offered. These drive:
- Product creation
- Copywriting angles
- Content marketing topics
- Email marketing campaigns

**Example (Agency Eric):**
- Goals: Increase agency business, increase team capabilities, scale business
- Values: Professional development, providing client value, "white hat" marketing

### 2. Sources of Information

Where the avatar gets their information. This determines:
- Best ad platforms to use
- Targeting options for paid traffic
- Content distribution channels

**The "But No One Else Would" Trick:**
Complete these sentences to find niche-specific sources:
- "My ideal customer would read [BOOK] but no one else would."
- "My ideal customer would subscribe to [MAGAZINE] but no one else would."
- "My ideal customer would attend [CONFERENCE] but no one else would."
- "My ideal customer would follow [GURU] but no one else would."

The goal is to find interests specific enough to target precisely. Avoid broad figures
(e.g., Tiger Woods for golf) — choose niche ones (e.g., Rory McIlroy) that let you
include ideal customers and exclude everyone else.

### 3. Demographic Information

Standard demographics bring the avatar to life:
- Age
- Gender
- Marital Status
- Number/Age of Children
- Location
- Occupation / Job Title
- Annual Income
- Level of Education

The **Quote** field is particularly powerful — it captures the avatar's mindset in their
own words and helps you "get inside their head" when writing copy.

### 4. Challenges & Pain Points

What keeps them up at night? This drives:
- New product/service development
- Copywriting and ad creative
- Content that resonates

**Example:**
- Challenge: Scaling agency business
- Pain Point: Fear of losing business to competitors
- Copy angle: "Are you tired of losing proposals simply because you don't offer
  Content Marketing services?"

### 5. Objections & Role in Purchase Process

Why would they NOT buy? These must be addressed in marketing.

**Example objections:**
- "Does this fit an existing service or a new one?"
- "How long will my team be out of pocket for training?"

**Role in purchase:**
- Are they the decision maker?
- Are they a decision influencer?
- Do they need approval from someone else?

## Example: Agency Eric (Complete Avatar)

**Demographics:**
- Age: 40 | Male | Married | 2 children (8 & 10)
- Location: Orlando, Florida
- Occupation: Digital Marketing | CEO/Founder
- Income: $150,000 | Education: MBA
- Quote: "I surround myself with people smarter than me."
- Other: Spends time on LinkedIn looking for talent

**Goals & Values:**
- Goals: Increase agency business, increase team capabilities, scale business
- Values: Professional development, providing client value, "white hat" marketing

**Sources of Information:**
- Books: Good to Great, Think & Grow Rich
- Magazines: Wired, Fast Company
- Blogs: AdAge, DigiDay, Social Fresh
- Conferences: Content Marketing World, SXSW, Inbound
- Gurus: Jay Baer, Joe Pulizzi, Christopher Penn

**Challenges & Pain Points:**
- Challenges: Scaling agency, finding/training/retaining talent, staying sharp as CEO
- Pain Points: Fear of losing to competitors, fear of falling behind in digital marketing

**Objections & Purchase Role:**
- Objections: Does training fit existing or new services? How long out of pocket?
- Role: Primary decision maker. Not price-sensitive if training provides competitive edge.

## Building Multiple Avatars

Start with one avatar, then build additional ones for distinct market segments. Each
segment with a unique set of goals, information sources, or pain points deserves its
own avatar. For this onboarding process, build three types:

1. **Buyer Persona** — The ideal purchasing customer
2. **Amplifier Persona** — People who can amplify the brand (influencers, partners, PR)
3. **Attention Persona** — Audience consuming content but not yet buying
