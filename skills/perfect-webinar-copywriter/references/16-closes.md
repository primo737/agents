# The 16 Closes (and Trial Closes)

When you reach the stack at the end of the presentation, you don't just ask for
the sale once — you layer several short "closes," each attacking a different
reason someone hesitates (money fear, time fear, identity, risk, inertia). You
don't use all 16 in one webinar. Pick the 4–6 that fit the specific audience and
argument, then always finish with the **Close Close**. Customize the blanks to
the user's offer, numbers, and promise.

## Trial closes (sprinkle throughout the whole presentation)

These keep the audience nodding and engaged before you ever pitch:
- Are you ready to get started?
- Are you all getting this?
- Is this making sense?
- Can you imagine if that happened to you?
- Who here wants a free copy of ______?
- Would you like to be our next case study?
- Isn't that cool? / Isn't that exciting? / Am I right?
- Can you see yourself doing ______?

## The 16 stack closes

**1. Money Is Good.** Reframe money as a neutral tool for exchange — you can't eat
it or stay warm with it, it only has value when traded for something you want
more. Every purchase happens because the buyer believes what they're getting is
worth more than the cash. "Would you exchange that money for these results? If
yes, get started now — and if it's not what you expected, we'll give it back."

**2. Disposable Income.** Most people live paycheck to paycheck, and the leftover
"disposable" money gets blown on short-term pleasures that vanish. Your job is to
help them redirect that money into something that actually moves them toward a
goal — an investment in products, programs, and services instead of ice cream and
movies.

**3. Money Replenishes (but time doesn't).** Money comes back every two weeks —
time never does. You can spend months or years figuring this out the hard way and
never get that time back, or invest money (which replenishes) to skip straight to
the result by working with someone who's already paid the time cost for you.

**4. Break Old Habits.** Habits are hard to change. "I could leave right now — I'm
already successful at this. But this isn't about me, it's about you. If you leave
now, by tomorrow morning you'll have slipped back into your old routine. As your
coach, I'm not going to let that happen. Real change needs repeated exposure to
the full system — that's what you get when you invest today."

**5. Information Alone.** "I've shown you the whole system. But information alone
won't make you successful — you'll need coaching, answers to your questions, and
accountability. My success rate for people going it alone is almost 0%; for people
who work with me it's closer to ___%. If information were enough, you could've
just used Google."

**6. Money or Excuses.** "There are only two kinds of people: those good at
[result] and those good at making excuses — you can't be both. The good news is
you get to choose, right now, which one you're going to be."

**7. Your Two Choices (the pricing story).** "When we priced this we had two
options: go as cheap as possible (which would force us to strip out value), or
raise the price a little and give you absolutely everything you need to succeed.
We chose the second."

**8. Their Two Choices.** "You've got two choices. Option one: do nothing (100%
risk-free, but nothing changes). Option two: make this tiny investment, give it a
real shot, and if it doesn't work for any reason, get your money back. There's no
risk — nothing to lose but the stress and headaches."

**9. The Hand Hold.** Literally walk them through buying: "Open a browser — Chrome,
Firefox, whatever. Go to [URL]. You'll see ___. Click here, fill out this form,
create your account. If you hit any snag, this link reaches my support desk."
Removes friction by making the next step concrete.

**10. Us vs. Them.** "Two kinds of people are listening: do-ers and dabblers.
Dabblers love to listen and learn but rarely act. Do-ers aren't sure exactly how
it'll work for them, but they've seen it work for others and have faith. It's the
do-ers who get ahead."

**11. Say Goodbye.** "Once you've been through this and have it set up, you can say
goodbye to [the pain] forever. Imagine what life is like when that stress has
disappeared. What will you do with all the extra time / energy / money?"

**12. Now & Later.** Paint the before ("Here's where I was — struggling with ___,
unable to ___") and the after ("Ever since [solution], I've been able to ___, and
things are amazing"). Let them see themselves making the same jump.

**13. Only Excuses.** Address the three reasons they didn't buy yet: (1) "you
think you can't do [X]" — we spend the whole first module on exactly that; (2)
"you're nervous about setup" — week 2 walks step-by-step; (3) "it's too
expensive" — this is an investment and a decision only you can make; when I
invested $___ I got back ___ within ___.

**14. Reluctant Hero.** "I'm no one special. I don't have supernatural gifts. I
actually really struggled with this — and that's exactly why I love this system,
because I don't have to worry about that anymore." Lowers the bar: if I can, you
can.

**15. If You Only Got That…** "I could stop right here. If all you got was [first
component], it'd still be worth the investment. But you're ALSO getting
[component 2], [component 3]…" — re-stacking to make the price feel trivial.

**16. Close Close.** "If you're still on the fence, now is the time. Open a new
browser window, go to [URL], and get started. Remember, there's no risk — 100%
money-back guarantee. The only way to know if this is right for you is to get
started right now."

When helping a user, ask which fears their specific audience has most (money?
self-doubt? past failure? overwhelm?) and recommend the matching closes.
