# Origin Story Builder

The origin story is the emotional foundation of the whole funnel. Before anyone
buys, they have to believe two things: that the thing you're selling works, and
that it can work *for them*. A good origin story collapses both objections at
once. It shows the audience that you were once where they are now — stuck,
skeptical, struggling — and that a specific discovery is what turned it around.
That discovery is your offer. Tell the story well and the pitch later feels like
the obvious next step rather than a hard sell.

Run this as a conversation. Ask the questions a few at a time (not all at once —
it's overwhelming), acknowledge each answer warmly, then assemble the story and
read it back. The user almost always wants to tweak the wording once they see it
assembled, so treat the first assembly as a draft.

## The questions to ask

1. What are you selling? (the product/offer this story leads into)
2. What's a negative event or circumstance you want to build the story around?
3. Turn that negative into a past event with a verb ending in "-ing" (e.g.
   "struggling to write sales copy").
4. What were you trying to do or accomplish at the time?
5. What were you struggling with at the time?
6. As a result, what negative emotion or circumstance was overtaking you?
7. What bad thing happened that threw you off track? (the moment it got worse)
8. What did you really need to be able to do but couldn't, when #7 happened?
9. What's the dream you weren't willing to give up on, no matter what?
10. What did you discover or find that turned everything around?
11. What did that discovery reveal to you? (collect 3 insights)
12. Big payoff #1 from the discovery.
13. Big payoff #2 from the discovery.
14. Big payoff #3 from the discovery.

## Assembly template

Fill the placeholders with the user's answers. Keep their voice; smooth the
grammar but don't sanitize the emotion.

**(Part 1 — Setup)**
> Let me tell you a quick story about [#2].
> At the time, I was trying to [#4].
> I was struggling with [#5].
> And I was getting more and more [#6].

**(Part 2 — Turn for the Worse)** (pick one opener)
> Then something really bad happened: [#7].
> *— or —*
> Then the bottom fell out from under me when [#7].
>
> Which meant I [#8].
> As you can imagine, I was in bad shape and desperate, but I wasn't ready to
> give up on [#9].

**(Part 3 — The Discovery)**
> Then I discovered / was introduced to / decided to [#10].
> At that point, everything changed! I finally figured out how to:
> - [#11 first insight]
> - [#11 second insight]
> - [#11 third insight]

**(Part 4 — The Transformation)**
> Because I [#10], I was able to:
> - [#12]
> - [#13]
> - [#14]
>
> Now I'm living my dream of [#9] and I never have to worry about [the struggle
> from #5] ever again.

**(Part 5 — The Close Transition)**
> And that's why I'm so passionate about [#1] — so you can experience [the
> benefits they'll get] too!

## Worked example (for reference)

Selling: Funnel Scripts. Negative event: trying to sell a brand-new product that
took months to create. Past-event "-ing": struggling to write sales copy.
Trying to: sell a new audio CD program. Struggling with: getting copy to
convert. Overtaking emotion: frustrated and scared. Bad thing: ran traffic from a
small list and nobody bought. Really needed: to keep working from home instead of
getting a "real job" again. Dream: using the internet to sell products and help
people. Discovery: sales-copy blueprints and templates. Revealed: model proven
copy patterns, test elements quickly, make copy fun and less stressful. Payoffs:
create all the copy he'd ever need, massively increase income, spread his message.

Notice how each answer is concrete and a little raw — that specificity is what
makes the assembled story land. Coach the user toward concrete answers, not
vague ones ("frustrated and scared" beats "it was hard").
