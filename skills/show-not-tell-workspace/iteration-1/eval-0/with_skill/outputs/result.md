The agency hit six figures before I had a single person to call and tell.

I remember sitting in the car after closing a deal I had been chasing for three months — and having no one to text. My friends had stopped following up around year two. My family was proud in a vague way. The team went home. I drove to a restaurant alone, ate at the bar, and made two decisions that night I would spend the next year untangling.

That's what nobody maps for you: not the all-nighters, not the payroll gaps, not the clients who disappear. It's the way winning starts to feel identical to losing when you have no one in the room who understands what you're actually carrying.

And isolated people make decisions like isolated people. They hire fast because they're desperate for company. They say yes to bad partnerships because rejection feels worse than a bad deal. They spend money they shouldn't because buying something feels like proof it's working.

I made every one of those moves.

The Bootstrap Entrepreneur exists because I found my way back from them — and because the pattern is recognizable before it's irreversible. By the time you're reading this, you'll know exactly which decision in your own business came from fear wearing a strategy mask.

That's the whole book. Learn to tell the difference before the deal is signed.
