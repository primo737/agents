There were nights I'd close a six-figure deal, hang up the phone, and sit in a silent office with no one to call.

Not because I didn't have people. Because none of them would understand what just happened — or why I wasn't sure how to feel about it.

I built the agency from a laptop and a list of cold contacts. Skipped dinners, missed weddings, said "soon" to everyone I loved until they stopped asking. That part I expected. What I didn't expect was winning — and still feeling like I was making every call alone.

That's where the bad decisions came from. Not from ignorance. From hunger. From needing to feel something other than the particular silence of a room where you've outgrown every person in it.

I wrote The Bootstrap Entrepreneur for the version of me who was already in that room and didn't know it yet.

It's not a hustle manual. It's a map for building something real without losing your footing — so the decisions you make at the top look the same as the ones you made at the start.
