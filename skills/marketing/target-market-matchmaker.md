# Target Market Matchmaker

Hey, we're starting with the Target Market Matchmaker, which is step one in the avatar sequence. It's the most important step. There are two types of people:

1. If you have an existing business, you might skip this step because you already know your target audience and the problems you solve for them. You're just looking to refine your messaging, product, and marketing strategy.
2. If you're new, considering a business model change, or starting a side hustle, this training is essential. It will help you identify who you should be helping, saving you time and effort in the future.

**Step One:** Write down who you are most passionate about helping long-term. Identify a defined group of people, known as an avatar, niche, or market segment, for whom you enjoy solving significant problems. List a few key problems you can solve for this audience. Which problem stands out as the most important? This is an initial guess, not extensive market research. You should have a general idea, like helping businesses increase sales or assisting people in losing weight.

If you have an idea or existing business, remember this is not your final answer. The process is about refinement, so document your thoughts as best as you can now. Consider the value of solving this problem for your audience. Who are they currently paying to solve it? As the saying goes, if you have a new idea or product, you’re either a fool or a genius. We want you to find a middle ground by identifying a target market with an existing, significant problem and solving it more effectively than others in the space.

Consider the potential worth of solving this problem. If it's only worth $10, scaling will be challenging. Look for a problem with substantial value because the cost of not solving it is high. Examine what people are currently paying to solve it. If there's an existing market where people are already paying, that's a positive sign. Estimate what you might charge for your program or product pathway. Determine where your target audience can be found. Are they at local events, requiring travel? Can you reach them through advertising on YouTube, Google, Facebook, or Twitter? This is not precise market research; we'll conduct that throughout the avatar process. For now, ensure there's a viable market you can reach online.

The Target Market Matchmaker is about taking action, even if imperfect. It's a preliminary look at who you should be helping—someone you're passionate about and can reach online.

# Avatar Snapshot

The Avatar Snapshot is the next crucial step. Many advanced marketers and agency owners often overlook this. By adopting a beginner’s mindset, they achieve better results. This isn't the glamorous part of marketing, but it's essential. Understanding customer demographics, psychographics, income, and beliefs is vital. In the original Fletcher Method training, the avatar framework hasn't changed much because the fundamental motivations and drivers remain the same across industries.

Demographics include name, gender, age, location, income, and marital status. Psychographics cover activities, patterns, and beliefs. Knowing who your audience follows, the groups they subscribe to, and their interests helps in marketing. By understanding these aspects upfront, every piece of content and ad will be more effective.

Determine where your avatar spends time—online or offline. Are they on LinkedIn, Reddit, or Facebook? We'll delve into audience insights and tools later, but for now, have an idea of where your avatar is being marketed to. You might be your own avatar, knowing who's advertising to you and where. Or, your business experience might provide this insight. Consider the outcome first; this is the initial step.

The framework is straightforward. Describe your target audience in one or two sentences. For example: "Jim is an entrepreneurial executive earning X per year. He enjoys Y and struggles with Z." This statement is your mission statement. Prove you know your avatar by detailing their demographic and psychographic attributes. Understand their motivations, beliefs, and hobbies. Identify where they spend time and how to reach them effectively.

# Avatar Goals

Understanding avatar goals is crucial for taking a product to market. Know the struggles people face, their current goals, and the long-term effects. Your avatar has goals and reasons for wanting to achieve them. For instance, they might want to lose 10 pounds to improve their appearance and meet someone special or live to see their children graduate. In business, they may want to scale their ad campaign from $10K to $50K monthly for financial stability.

Every market, consumer or business, has goals and reasons for pursuing them. They also face obstacles. Consider your target market's goals and the barriers they face. Think of these barriers as bricks in a wall. Identify the three main obstacles preventing them from achieving their goals and the consequences of not overcoming them. For example, in the B2C space, if someone wants to lose 10 pounds, obstacles might include lack of time, gym intimidation, and low motivation. Failure to overcome these could lead to health risks or missed life opportunities.

In marketing, obstacles might include technical overwhelm, lack of funds for an agency, or time constraints. Not addressing these could result in business failure. The original avatar framework focuses on current pains, goals, and the consequences of inaction. We've simplified this in the app. List the top three short-term goals your avatar is trying to achieve, ensuring they relate to your product category. Consider their aspirations in your business category, whether financial advising, personal training, or marketing.

Identify the top three obstacles preventing your avatar from achieving their goals. Where can you find this information? Look at Amazon book reviews, competitor ads, blog posts, and discussions. Engage with your avatar to understand their needs. You don't need a deep-dive survey; a little research in an existing market can provide insights. Determine the main reasons your customers face challenges and their worst-case fears if these aren't addressed.
